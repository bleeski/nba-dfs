# NBA DFS Engine Brief: Adversarial Critique

## Verdict
No: as written, the brief and its accompanying `ARCHITECTURE.md` (version 3) will ship a safe file-validator wrapped around a miscalibrated model that misses both goals. Goal 1 optimizes a flat binary top-1% threshold that treats 352nd place ($50) the same as 1st place ($200,000), while Goal 2 ("zero cashes across the night") is mathematically inert at your 50-to-200-entry volume and suffers from a standard-error formula bug in the reference spike (`spikes/night/night_spike.py`) that freezes 10 of 11 contests. Worse, `ARCHITECTURE.md`'s default "permissive" data posture cuts off confirmed starting lineups, live box scores, and nightly player props, while its Opening Night runbook freezes LLM research on Monday evening and misses all Tuesday game-day injury and starter news before the 2:00 PM CT lock. Finally, trying to build 25 components across 22 decision records in six days while juggling 30 to 40 manual file downloads and re-downloads per night repeats the exact over-scoping and operator-fatigue traps that stalled your prior MLB, NFL, and NHL engines. Fix the objective math, drop the fragile stored-replacement ledger in favor of fast live re-solves, adopt the personal-use data stack (`nba_api` plus ESPN JSON), and cut Phase 1 to a five-part walking skeleton before writing a line of production code.

## Top five
1. **I-01:** Goal 1's binary top-1% coverage objective starves first-place equity in large-field GPPs and ignores entry fees
2. **I-02:** Goal 2 ("zero cashes across the night") is degenerate at 50+ entries, and the insurance search (`ADR-001`, `night_spike.py`) has a standard-error bug that freezes 10 of 11 contests
3. **I-03:** The six-day Phase 1 build scope and Monday-night Opening Night freeze (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §8, §13) will miss Tuesday game-day injury and starter news
4. **I-04:** Stored replacement sets (`dfs apply`, `ARCHITECTURE.md` §6, `ADR-011`) cannot compose when two players resolve on the same slate, and solve a non-existent solver bottleneck
5. **I-05:** The "permissive" data posture (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §10, `ADR-005`) leaves the engine blind to confirmed starters, current-season box scores, and nightly props, while triggering a field-model edge leak

## Issues

### I-01: Goal 1's binary top-1% coverage objective starves first-place equity in large-field GPPs and ignores entry fees
**Severity:** Blocker · **Affects:** Goal 1 · **Confidence:** High · **Applies to:** Brief, `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§2, §6), `ADR-001`, `ADR-007`

**What it is.** The brief defines Goal 1 as: *"Within each contest, maximize the probability of a top-1% finish. For a single-entry contest, that's the probability our lineup finishes in the top 1%. For a multi-entry contest, it's the probability that at least one of our entries does."* `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§2, §6) and `ADR-007` implement this literally via greedy scenario coverage (**submodular optimization**, a method that adds lineups one at a time based on how many new simulated nights they clear a target score in). The brief never mentions **expected value (EV)** (average dollar profit per dollar risked), **return on investment (ROI)**, or contest entry fees.

**Why it's worth solving.** In large DraftKings **guaranteed prize pool (GPP)** tournaments (contests where payouts are heavily concentrated in the top few spots), the top-1% cutoff pays a tiny fraction of first place. Verified in `R6-draftkings-mechanics.md` (§3.1, §5), DraftKings' flagship Opening Tip Off ($20 entry, 35,294 entries, $600,000 prize pool) pays $200,000 (33.3% of the pool, or 10,000x the buy-in) to 1st place, while 352nd place (the top-1% cutoff) pays roughly 2x to 4x the entry fee. Under binary top-1% coverage, finishing 352nd and finishing 1st receive the exact same weight (`1.0`). Once one of your 150-max lineups clears the 99th-percentile score (`~338` DraftKings points in `R9-night-benchmark.md`) in a simulated game script, the greedy selector assigns **zero marginal value** to any second lineup that would score `385` points and win $200,000 in that same script. It systematically steers multi-entry portfolios toward medium-ceiling lineups that barely clear the 99th percentile and away from the extreme tail outcomes that pay for the rest of the season. This is not theoretical: in `RR3-nhl-golf-soccer-lessons.md` (`B110`, `B112`), your NHL engine used this exact top-1% coverage objective and cashed 23 of 136 Classic entries (`-62.5%` ROI versus `32.2` expected random cashes) with 0 of 135 top-1% finishes. Furthermore, ignoring entry fees treats a $0.25 20-max contest ($5 total risk) identically to a $20 150-max contest ($3,000 total risk).

**Recommended solution and why.** Keep the top-1% cutoff as the support boundary (so min-cashes never pollute Goal 1), but weight each top-1% finish by its **duplication-adjusted share of the top-1% prize pool** (or, when a full payout table is unavailable, a convex power of rank between the 99th percentile and 1st place). As Haugh and Singal (2021) prove (`R2-optimization-methods.md` §5.2), maximizing expected top-tier payout over best-scoring entries is still a monotone submodular ("facility location") objective, so the exact same fast greedy algorithm applies with its `1 - 1/e` (about 63%) quality guarantee intact, while properly rewarding 1st-place upside and allowing multiple entries to attack the same high-payout game script at different ranks. Replace Goal 1 in the brief with:
> 1. Within each contest, maximize **top-1% prize equity**: the expected share of the contest's top-1% prize pool won by our entries, after splitting tied payouts with duplicate opponent lineups. Finishes outside the top 1% (or outside 1st place in fields under 100 entries) score zero inside Goal 1. Across contests, weight diagnostics and portfolio tradeoffs by total dollar entry fees at risk.

### I-02: Goal 2 ("zero cashes across the night") is degenerate at 50+ entries, and the insurance search (`ADR-001`, `night_spike.py`) has a standard-error bug that freezes 10 of 11 contests
**Severity:** Blocker · **Affects:** Goal 2, Both goals · **Confidence:** High · **Applies to:** Brief, `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§2, §6, §14 `D3`), `ADR-001`, `ADR-007`, `spikes/night/night_spike.py`

**What it is.** The brief defines Goal 2 as: *"Across the night's portfolio, minimize the probability of a total washout. A washout is zero cashing entries across every contest that night, Classic and Showdown combined."* In `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§2 line 139) and `ADR-001`, the insurance search trades off Goal 1 for Goal 2 using the constraint:
`G1_c(new) >= (1 - epsilon) x G1*_c + 2 x SE_paired(change in G1_c)`
and cites `R9-night-benchmark.md` (`EDGE-30k`) to set the default budget `epsilon = 2%` (`Decision D3`). Inspection of `spikes/night/night_spike.py` (lines 957-970), which `ADR-022` designates as the reference implementation and test oracle, reveals that both the brief's definition and the spike's math are broken.

**Why it's worth solving.** Three things go wrong at once:
1. **The brief's washout definition ignores dollar loss.** Under "zero cashing entries across every contest," if you enter $1,000 of Main Slate contests and one $0.25 Showdown entry, and only the $0.25 entry min-cashes for $0.40, the engine counts the night as a non-washout (`cntW > 0` in `night_spike.py` line 971) even though you lost 99.96% of your bankroll that night.
2. **At your planned volume (50 to 200 entries), zero-cash washout is near zero in the simulator.** In `R9` (lines 214-222, 383), a 60-entry 150-max portfolio cashed at least one entry in all 30,000 scenarios (`P(W) = 0.000%`), even with symmetric model error and edge uncertainty turned on. As `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§1 line 46, §2 line 148) admits, the insurance control does nothing at your target volume.
3. **The safety margin in `night_spike.py` (line 970) and `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§2 line 139) is mathematically wrong.** In `night_spike.py` lines 968-970:
   `se_rel(c) = math.sqrt(p_ * (1 - p_) / S) / p_`
   `thr = [math.ceil((1 - eps + margin_k * se_rel(c)) * g1star[c] - 1e-9) for c in range(nC)]`
   That `se_rel(c)` is the **unpaired** relative **Monte Carlo standard error** (simulation sampling noise) of `G1*_c`, not the paired standard error of the swap change. On `S_V = 15,000` validation scenarios, any contest with `G1*_c < 38%` has `2 * se_rel(c) > 0.02`. At `eps = 0.02` (`2%`) and `margin_k = 2.0`, the required threshold `(0.98 + 2 * se_rel(c)) * G1*_c` is **strictly greater than `1.00 * G1*_c`** for 10 of the 11 contests on the synthetic night (every contest except `C_150max8000`, where `G1* = 72%`). Because `G1*_c` is already the maximum found on that scenario set, the `margin_k = 2.0` run in `R9` accidentally froze 10 of 11 contests and only swapped inside the 150-max. Even if replaced with `SE_paired(Delta_c)`, writing `(1 - epsilon) * G1*_c + 2 * SE_paired` makes any swap whose `2 * SE_paired > epsilon * G1*_c` require `G1_c(new) > G1*_c`, which is impossible.

**Recommended solution and why.** Redefine Goal 2 in the brief around **dollar fee recovery** rather than "zero cashing entries anywhere," and fix the lower confidence bound in `ARCHITECTURE.md` and `ADR-001`:
1. **Brief replacement text for Goal 2:**
   > 2. Across the night's portfolio, minimize the probability of a **severe drawdown night**, defined as recovering less than `alpha` (default `25%`) of total nightly entry fees across all Classic and Showdown contests combined. (Setting `alpha = 0.01%` recovers the literal zero-cash definition on thin nights.)
2. **Math fix in `ARCHITECTURE.md` §2 and `ADR-001`:** A swap from baseline portfolio `E*_c` to `E_c` has paired difference `Delta_c = G1_c(E_c) - G1_c(E*_c)` on the validation set, with paired standard error `SE_paired(Delta_c)`. The lower 95% confidence bound on the change is `Delta_c - 2 * SE_paired(Delta_c)`. The correct non-degradation constraint is:
   `Delta_c - 2 * SE_paired(Delta_c) >= -epsilon * G1_c(E*_c)`
   which is `G1_c(E_c) >= (1 - epsilon) * G1_c(E*_c) + 2 * SE_paired(Delta_c)` **only when evaluated per incremental swap where `SE_paired(0) = 0`**, and must never substitute unpaired `se_rel(c)` as `night_spike.py` line 970 did.

### I-03: The six-day Phase 1 build scope and Monday-night Opening Night freeze (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §8, §13) will miss Tuesday game-day injury and starter news
**Severity:** Blocker · **Affects:** Feasibility, Both goals, Workload · **Confidence:** High · **Applies to:** Brief, `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§8, §13, §14 `D7`), `ADR-016`

**What it is.** Today is Tuesday, October 13, 2026; NBA Opening Night is Tuesday, October 20, 2026. Because `R4-data-sources.md` (§4.10) and `R6-draftkings-mechanics.md` (§3.1) observed `BOS @ DET` listed at `3:00 PM ET` (`2:00 PM CT`) on Tuesday, October 20, `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§8 lines 729-740, `Decision D7`) schedules the full LLM research and build for **Monday evening, October 19**, followed by a no-LLM run (`dfs night --no-llm --reuse-overrides`) at `12:30 PM CT` on Tuesday. Meanwhile, `ARCHITECTURE.md` §13 schedules a 25-component build across 22 ADRs in six days (Oct 14 to Oct 19).

**Why it's worth solving.** Two things break on Opening Night:
1. **Monday-night research misses all game-day NBA news.** Under official NBA rules (`R4` §4.3, verified from `official.nba.com`), teams do not submit game-day injury reports until `11:00 AM to 1:00 PM` local time on game day (`8:00 to 10:00 AM` local for tips at or before 5:00 PM), coaches announce opening-night minutes restrictions at Tuesday morning shootaround, and confirmed starting lineups post `0 to 75 minutes` before tip (`R4` §4.9). Running the LLM news researcher on Monday night and freezing `--no-llm --reuse-overrides` on Tuesday afternoon guarantees your Opening Night portfolio ignores every game-day injury upgrade/downgrade, shootaround quote, and surprise starter across all three games (`BOS @ DET`, `PHI @ NYK`, `OKC @ SAS`). (Also note: a `3:00 PM ET` Tuesday tip on NBA Opening Night is highly unusual and likely a pre-broadcast-lock placeholder time in the schedule feed before TNT/NBC evening tip times finalize; if `BOS @ DET` is actually an evening tip, the entire Monday-night workaround was solving a schedule artifact.)
2. **Six-day build scope repeats your prior repos' failure mode.** In `nfl-dfs` (`RR2-nfl-dfs-lessons.md` Bottom Line #1), an ambitious simulation-and-field roadmap sat `Pending` for six weeks while 59,000 lines of safety and governance plumbing were built and real slates were run by hand passes. In `dfs-architect` (`RR4-dfsos-nbabetting-lessons.md` §4.1), 12,394 lines of library code were written in three days, and the first live slate halted because there was no working entry point. Building DuckDB stores, per-entry state ledgers, precomputed replacement stores, Bluesky daemons, and four-set scenario pipelines in six days while sharing the same weekly Claude Pro usage window needed to run Opening Night is a high-probability deadline miss.

**Recommended solution and why.**
1. **Run the LLM researcher on game day, not the night before.** On Tuesday, October 20, run a tight, 3-minute LLM news digest pass (`max 6 WebSearch calls`) 45 to 60 minutes before the first actual lock (after morning shootarounds and the game-day injury PDF land). Check the live NBA schedule on Monday, October 19 to confirm whether `BOS @ DET` is a real 2:00 PM CT tip or a 6:00/6:30 PM CT evening tip.
2. **Cut Phase 1 (Oct 14 to Oct 19) to a 5-file walking skeleton:** (a) DraftKings CSV parser + validator + exporter, (b) `nba_api` / ESPN JSON / Official Injury PDF adapters + market-anchored projections, (c) stat-level simulator + Showdown enumerator + GLPK Hall-formulation candidate solver, (d) greedy portfolio selector, and (e) fast live re-solve CLI (`dfs night`, `dfs swap`). Defer the DuckDB database (use Parquet/JSON files in Week 1), the per-entry state ledger, the stored replacement composer, the Bluesky background daemon, and the 3-layer edge-uncertainty model to Phase 2.

### I-04: Stored replacement sets (`dfs apply`, `ARCHITECTURE.md` §6, `ADR-011`) cannot compose when two players resolve on the same slate, and solve a non-existent solver bottleneck
**Severity:** Blocker · **Affects:** Both goals, Feasibility, Workload · **Confidence:** High · **Applies to:** `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§3, §6, §8), `ADR-011`

**What it is.** After Red Team Round 2 (`RT2-red-team-round2.md` Finding 1) pointed out that pre-written "if-out" upload CSVs overwrite each other when two questionable players resolve in different games, `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§6 lines 515-518) and `ADR-011` replaced them with **Component 15 (`Replacement store and dfs apply`)**: at build time, the engine precomputes 2-to-3-slot replacement tuples per affected Classic entry (and alternate Showdown portfolios) for both "he sits" and "he plays," and when news breaks, `dfs apply` *"combines every confirmed change against the ledger, in any order, and writes one file per slate in seconds."*

**Why it's worth solving.** Precomputed multi-slot replacement tuples on a $50,000 salary-cap knapsack **cannot be composed additively** when two or more news events occur on the same Classic slate or in the same Showdown game:
1. **Player and salary collisions:** Suppose Entry #7 holds Questionable Player X ($8,200 PG) and Questionable Player Y ($6,800 SF), with $300 of unused salary cap. At build time, X's 2-slot replacement swaps `(X, RolePlayer_Z) -> (Pivot_A, Pivot_B)` using $200 of that cap room. Independently, Y's 2-slot replacement swaps `(Y, RolePlayer_Z) -> (Pivot_A, Pivot_C)` using $300 of that cap room. When both X and Y are ruled out (or X is ruled out and Y is ruled in), `dfs apply` cannot combine those two replacements: both modify `RolePlayer_Z`, both insert `Pivot_A` (creating an illegal duplicate player in Entry #7), and together they exceed the $50,000 salary cap by $200.
2. **Non-linear team redistribution and Showdown combinatorics:** On a typical NBA night (`R4` §4.3 found 12 Questionable players on a single report), a single game or Showdown slate often has 2 to 4 Questionable players. Three Questionable players in a Showdown game create `2^3 = 8` joint availability states, not 6 marginal states. If two teammates sit together, usage and minutes flow to a third player whom neither marginal "X sits" nor "Y sits" replacement priced.
3. **It solves a phantom bottleneck.** Why did `ARCHITECTURE.md` invent precomputed replacement sets in the first place? Because the narrow re-run took `103 seconds` at `S = 30,000` (`44 seconds` at `S = 10,000`). Yet `R9-night-benchmark.md` (lines 134-148) proved that re-simulating the affected game took `1.0 second`, replacing Classic MILP candidates took `2.4 seconds`, Showdown re-screening took `0.5 seconds`, and greedy selection took `0.3 seconds`. Over `71%` (`73.1 seconds`) of the narrow re-run went to recomputing field quantiles from scratch across 30,000 scenarios.

**Recommended solution and why.** Delete Component 15 (`Replacement store and dfs apply`) and the precomputed replacement queue entirely. Instead, when news breaks (`dfs out "Player"` or `dfs in "Player"`), run a **live incremental re-solve** (see **D-06**): re-simulate only the affected game (`1.0s`), update opponent field scores incrementally by adding only that game's score delta (`<0.5s`), re-solve affected Classic entries in GLPK (`<3s`) and re-enumerate affected Showdowns (`<1s`). A live solver run finishes in **under 10 seconds**, handles any combination of simultaneous `OUT` and `IN` rulings across any number of games, respects exact current lock clocks, and can never collide on salary or duplicate players.

### I-05: The "permissive" data posture (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §10, `ADR-005`) leaves the engine blind to confirmed starters, current-season box scores, and nightly props, while triggering a field-model edge leak
**Severity:** Blocker · **Affects:** Both goals, Feasibility, Workload · **Confidence:** High · **Applies to:** Brief, `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§5, §10, §14 `D1`), `ADR-005`, `ADR-008`, `R4-data-sources.md`

**What it is.** The brief says: *"Flag any source whose terms prohibit what we'd do with it, and respect rate limits."* In response, `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§10, `Decision D1`) and `ADR-005` recommend a **"permissive" default posture** that bans `nba_api` (`stats.nba.com`), ESPN's public JSON API (`site.api.espn.com`), and RotoWire, leaving the engine dependent on `sportsdataverse` static files, the DraftKings salary CSV `Starting` column, The Odds API's 500-credit free tier, and manual browser downloads of Daily Fantasy Fuel CSVs.

**Why it's worth solving.** Under the default permissive posture, four critical inputs fail in production:
1. **No confirmed starting lineups:** `R1-platforms.md` (Finding 1) and `R4-data-sources.md` (§4.9) prove that NBA DFS slates are decided by minutes and late starting-lineup confirmations (in `R4`'s live test, the Knicks replaced 3 of 5 expected starters right before tip). The permissive posture relies on DraftKings' `Starting` column, which `RR4-dfsos-nbabetting-lessons.md` (§1.4) verified **did not exist in your April 15, 2026 NBA Classic salary CSV**, plus an unofficial Bluesky mirror bot (`shamsbot.bsky.social`) that mirrors national scoops, not 30-team starting lineups.
2. **Stale current-season box scores:** `sportsdataverse` parquet files had zero 2026 preseason games as of October 13 (last updated September 9, 2026, `R4` §4.7) and rely on a volunteer daily job. If that job lags by even 24 hours in October, the permissive posture has zero current-season minutes or usage data.
3. **Silent field-model edge leak (`ADR-008`):** The public field view (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §5 line 415) relies on Daily Fantasy Fuel's free projections CSV. Because Daily Fantasy Fuel's table loads via JavaScript (`0.0` in raw HTML, `R4` §4.6), `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§10 line 867) asks you to manually download that CSV in a browser. Whenever you skip that manual download, `ARCHITECTURE.md` §5 states: *"Our own baseline model is used only as a fallback when no consensus source has numbers."* That immediately triggers the **RT1 Rank 4 bug**: the simulated opponent field is generated from your own private baseline projections, turning your model's edge into simulated chalk and causing the optimizer to fade its own best plays.
4. **No player props on 75% of nights:** The Odds API's 500 monthly credits cover player props on only about 7 nights a month (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §10 line 866).

**Recommended solution and why.** Make the **low-volume personal-use posture (`nba_api` + ESPN public JSON + Official NBA Injury PDF + The Odds API)** the default for local single-user operation (`Decision D1`), while keeping DraftKings 100% manual (`ADR-004` is right to protect your real-money DraftKings account). `R4` (§4.1, §4.4, §4.9) verified from your laptop that `nba_api` (`PlayerGameLogs`, `PlayByPlayV3`, `ScheduleLeagueV2` in `<0.5s`) and ESPN public JSON (`site.api.espn.com` scoreboard odds, injuries, depth charts, and `propBets` DraftKings milestone ladders in `<0.5s`) work with zero blocks at 1 request every 2 seconds (~50 requests per night). Second, in `ADR-008`, **never** allow the public field view to fall back to the engine's private baseline model; if an external consensus CSV is missing, build the public view from a deterministic **Casual Value Formula** (`0.6 * Vegas_Implied_Team_Share + 0.4 * Trailing_5_Game_DK_Pts`, weighted by `Salary`).

### I-06: The reference simulator (`night_spike.py`, `ADR-002`, `ADR-022`) clamps team points before allocating player baskets, creating fake negative teammate correlation, and grades against a strawman field (`22%` max chalk)
**Severity:** Major · **Affects:** Both goals · **Confidence:** High · **Applies to:** `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§4, §5), `ADR-002`, `ADR-022`, `R9-night-benchmark.md`, `spikes/night/night_spike.py`, `NBA_DraftKings_DAG_Expanded.md`

**What it is.** `ADR-022` establishes `spikes/night/night_spike.py` as the reference implementation and test oracle (`"the same synthetic inputs must give the same numbers"`). However, `night_spike.py` violates the causal structure of the attached `NBA_DraftKings_DAG_Expanded.md` (§1, §3.2) and tests the portfolio against an unrealistically flat opponent field.

**Why it's worth solving.** Two flaws in `night_spike.py` corrupt both goals:
1. **Top-down point clamping (`lines 364-380`):** In `NBA_DraftKings_DAG_Expanded.md` (§3.2, §3.3), game pace sets team **possessions**, players split **opportunities** (shot attempts, free-throw attempts, rebound chances), and **conversions** (shooting percentages) turn opportunities into points. In `night_spike.py`, the code instead draws the team's final score `Pt_eff` first (`line 364`), draws team 3-pointers `n3` and free throws `nF`, and hard-clamps team 2-point makes to `n2 = round((Pt_eff + eps_t - 3*n3 - nF) / 2)` (`line 376`), then divides made baskets across players via a zero-sum multinomial draw (`_alloc`). That means whenever a star shoots efficiently and makes extra shots, his teammates are forced to make *fewer* shots even at fixed pace and usage! At the same time, the per-player gamma multiplier (`line 365`) plus lognormal belief error inflates star standard deviation to `30% to 34%` of the mean (`17.9` SD on a `53.1` mean, `99th percentile = 103.7`, `R9` line 173), compared to `22% to 25%` in real NBA box scores (`R9` line 187).
2. **Strawman opponent field (`lines 669-695`, `R9` line 190):** The casual opponent sampler (`classic_sampler`) produces a maximum player ownership of only `22%` on an 8-game slate (real NBA injury-replacement chalk routinely hits `45% to 70%`, `R5-nba-dfs-strategy.md` §2), and every "optimizer" opponent adds `15%` random noise to projections. Against this weak field, the synthetic engine cashes `31% to 44%` of single entries (versus `22%` paid and your real `13.2%` NBA cash rate in `R8-user-nba-history.md`), making top-1% odds look artificially high and washout risk look like zero.

**Recommended solution and why.** Amend `ADR-022` so that `night_spike.py` is used only as a **timing and bitset-mechanics benchmark, never as a statistical oracle**. Implement the simulator strictly bottom-up per `NBA_DraftKings_DAG_Expanded.md` §3.2:
1. Draw game pace (possessions per team) and competitive margin / overtime.
2. Allocate active minutes (reconciled to `240 + 25 * n_ot`) and allocate team **usage possessions** (`FGA + 0.44 * FTA + TOV`) across active players via Dirichlet-multinomial shares.
3. Draw individual player **makes** (`3PM`, `2PM`, `FTM`) as overdispersed binomials given each player's attempts and shrunk conversion rates (adjusted by a team offensive-efficiency factor), so a hot shooting night by a star increases team points rather than stealing made baskets from teammates.
4. Allocate rebounds from total simulated **missed shots** (`(FGA - FGM)` of both teams) and assists from **team `FGM`**, then calibrate player variance (`SD / mean ~ 0.22 to 0.25` for 34-minute starters) and field softmax temperature (`max Classic chalk 40% to 60%`) on 2025-26 historical slates before Oct 20.

### I-07: The duplication adjustment in the reference spike (`night_spike.py`, `ADR-009`) uses the flat `E[1/(1+D)]` formula that `ADR-009` explicitly rejects, throwing out 60% to 100% of top Showdown lineups
**Severity:** Major · **Affects:** Goal 1 · **Confidence:** High · **Applies to:** `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§2, §6, §14 `D2`), `ADR-009`, `R9-night-benchmark.md`, `spikes/night/night_spike.py`

**What it is.** `ADR-009` and `ARCHITECTURE.md` §2 (line 108) state that a flat `1/(1+D)` duplication penalty is wrong because *"a tie deep in the top 1%, where neighboring prizes are nearly equal, keeps nearly all of it,"* and specify that `share_c(L, s)` must average the prize split over the contest's payout table. Yet `spikes/night/night_spike.py` (line 917 `greedy_weighted` and line 1176 `share_factor`) implements the exact flat `f[L] = E[1/(1+D_L)]` penalty across the entire top-1% region, and `ARCHITECTURE.md` (§6 line 536) and `ADR-009` cite those exact spike numbers to justify `Decision D2`.

**Why it's worth solving.** In a 1,200-entry Showdown 20-max contest (`k_c = 12`), tying with 2 opponents at 1st place splits 1st, 2nd, and 3rd prizes (a huge dollar penalty), whereas tying with 2 opponents at 11th place splits 11th, 12th, and 13th prizes (which pay nearly identical amounts). Because `night_spike.py` multiplied every top-1% hit by `E[1/(1+D_L)]` regardless of rank, it treated a 2-copy tie at 11th place as losing two-thirds of the value. As a result, `greedy_weighted` threw out **6, 9, and 10 of the 10** raw Showdown picks (`R9` line 337) in favor of contrarian, lower-scoring lineups. In your real NFL Showdown standings (`RR2-nfl-dfs-lessons.md` Bottom Line #4, §7), `83% to 98%` of top-1% entries were lineups shared 6 or more times, and forcing unique or low-duplicate Showdown lineups was the worst-performing feature in every game.

**Recommended solution and why.** Fix `ADR-009` and the selector so duplication is never applied as a flat `E[1/(1+D_L)]` multiplier on a binary top-1% hit. Instead, use a 5-point piecewise payout curve `W_c(rank)` for each contest class (exact from pasted/historical tables when present: e.g., 1st, 2nd, 5th, top 0.5%, top 1%). In scenario `s`, when lineup `L` finishes at opponent rank `r <= k_c` and has expected opponent copy rate `q_L`, compute the expected prize won by averaging `sum_{j=0}^{D} W_c(r + j) / (1 + D)` over `D ~ Poisson(N'_c * q_L)`. This automatically penalizes duplication heavily at rank 1 while barely penalizing ties near rank `k_c`.

### D-01: Post-contest "Contest Flashback" simulator on real standings lineups
**Affects:** Both goals, Learning loop · **Effort:** Low · **Confidence:** High

**What it is.** Every morning when you ingest a contest's `contest-standings-<id>.zip` file, run the night simulator (`30,000` scenarios) against the **actual opponent lineups parsed from that standings file** under two settings: (1) **Pre-Game Flashback** (using your pre-lock projections and distributions as they stood at lock) and (2) **Hindsight-Role Flashback** (fixing each player's availability and minutes to what actually happened in the box score, while simulating per-minute stat conversion and game pace).

**Why it's worth solving.** As `R3-nba-modeling.md` (Summary #9) and `RR2-nfl-dfs-lessons.md` (Bottom Line #9) proved, grading portfolio strategy on actual contest finishes takes `88 to 328 slates` because only one box-score world happens each night (if a 5% owned player hits overtime, any lineup holding him wins; if a 45% chalk player gets hurt in Q1, any lineup holding him dies). Re-simulating 30,000 nights against the *real* opponent lineups (the free equivalent of SaberSim's $297/month Contest Flashback and Stokastic's Post-Contest Simulator, `R1-platforms.md` §3.1, §3.2) strips out single-game shooting and injury luck and tells you within **5 to 10 slates** whether your portfolio construction, duplication penalty, and LLM overrides actually beat the simple control portfolio against real human fields.

**Recommended solution and why.** Add a `flashback` step to `/results` (`Component 21` in `ARCHITECTURE.md` §3 and §9). Because the standings parser already maps every opponent lineup to player IDs and the candidate scorer already scores lineups in chunks (`R9` took `6.6s` to score lineups across 30,000 scenarios), scoring our portfolio, the pre-override portfolio, the pre-QA portfolio, and the control portfolio against the real opponent lineups takes under `15 seconds` per slate every morning. Use Hindsight-Role Flashback to isolate whether a bad night came from bad minutes projections or bad portfolio construction.

### D-02: Late-swap "Locked-Field Conditioning" using live mid-slate standings exports
**Affects:** Both goals · **Effort:** Medium · **Confidence:** High

**What it is.** On any Classic slate where late news breaks for the 9:00 PM or 10:00 PM ET games, download the live `contest-standings-<id>.zip` for your main contest after the 7:00 PM ET games lock (alongside your re-downloaded `DKEntries.csv`). In the standings file, every opponent's players from started games are visible with their live scores and actual `%Drafted` ownership, while unstarted slots read `LOCKED` (`R6-draftkings-mechanics.md` §2.4). Condition the late-swap field model on those exact locked opponent cores.

**Why it's worth solving.** Before lock, you have to guess opponent ownership and how opponents combined early-game players. One minute after the 7:00 PM ET tip, **half the puzzle is public fact**: you know the exact score, exact locked players, exact remaining salary cap (`$50,000` minus locked players' salaries), and exact remaining open roster slots for every single opponent in your contest (`R1` §3.2 Stokastic Live Sims). If a late scratch creates a `$3,500` value starter in the 10:00 PM game, Locked-Field Conditioning tells the engine exactly what fraction of your opponents even have a valid open slot and salary structure to swap to him, and knows which of your own entries are currently ahead of the pace (needing high-floor chalk blocks to secure a top-1% finish) versus behind the pace (needing correlated overtime or blowout stacks to catch the top 1%).

**Recommended solution and why.** Extend `/swap` (`Component 17`) to accept an optional mid-slate standings zip. For each opponent entry in the standings file, parse the visible locked players, compute remaining cap `Cap_rem` and remaining open positions `Pos_rem`, and sample the opponent's remaining `LOCKED` slots from the post-news public view conditioned on `(Cap_rem, Pos_rem)`. For games already `Final` or `In Progress`, set simulated variance to zero (or scale remaining-minutes variance by `TimeRemaining / 240`).

### D-03: Game-script conditional rotation regimes from play-by-play stint data
**Affects:** Both goals · **Effort:** Medium · **Confidence:** High

**What it is.** Instead of modeling each player's minutes as a continuous normal draw with a linear blowout trim (`night_spike.py` lines 318-362), model each team's minutes using **four discrete rotation regimes** fitted from `PlayByPlayV3` / `sportsdataverse` substitution stints (`R4` §4.1): (1) **Competitive Regulation** (starters play their normal 1st/3rd/closing stints; 8-to-9-man rotation), (2) **Overtime** (the 5 closing-lineup players each gain `+4.5 to +5.0` minutes per OT), (3) **Blowout (`|margin| > 16`)** (the 5 starters lose their entire 6-to-8-minute 4th-quarter closing stint together, and deep-bench players 9 to 13 enter as a 5-man unit), and (4) **Foul/Injury Early Exit** (one starter loses 12 to 24 minutes, absorbed directly by his primary positional backup).

**Why it's worth solving.** In `night_spike.py` (line 352), a blowout trims every starter's minutes by a smooth fraction (`1 - 0.013 * excess`) and adds a small amount to deep bench players, while independent normal noise (`line 319`) lets a starter and his direct positional backup both draw high minutes in the same normal-game scenario. In real NBA games (`R5-nba-dfs-strategy.md` §1, §3), a starter and his direct backup are strongly negatively correlated in competitive games (`-0.38 to -0.40` in `R5` §1), whereas in a 22-point blowout, *all five starters sit the 4th quarter together* and *both teams' deep bench units play 8 to 12 minutes together*. Capturing discrete 5-man closing and mop-up stints is what lets the Showdown enumerator discover winning `4-2` or `3-3` blowout bench builds and avoid dead starter-plus-backup pairs in competitive builds.

**Recommended solution and why.** In `Component 7 (`Availability, minutes and usage`)`, parse the last 10 games of substitution play-by-play for each team to label each player's role: `Starter_Closing`, `Starter_NonClosing`, `Primary_Backup_Pos`, and `MopUp_Bench`. In each simulated scenario, use the drawn game margin and overtime flag to select the regime first, then draw stint minutes within that regime so starter/backup exclusion and 4th-quarter bench correlations happen jointly.

### D-04: Contest-entry assignment optimizer ("which lineup goes to which contest")
**Affects:** Both goals · **Effort:** Low · **Confidence:** High

**What it is.** Add a final deterministic **linear assignment step** inside `Component 14 (`Portfolio selector`)` that assigns your chosen pool of lineups to specific contest `Entry ID` rows based on each contest's field size, entry limit, and payout flatness, rather than filling contests sequentially or in CSV row order.

**Why it's worth solving.** In `RR2-nfl-dfs-lessons.md` (§3.3, §7 item 14), your NFL engine assigned lineups in template order, which repeatedly put the chalkiest lineup into Winner-Take-All contests and the weakest lineups into satellites. Even within the same slate, a 50-entry Single Entry contest (where Goal 1 = 1st place only), a 1,200-entry 20-max, and a 35,000-entry 150-max reward very different duplication and variance profiles. Furthermore, when you hold multiple single-entry contests on the same slate (`Decision D5`), deciding whether to enter the same best lineup across two single-entry contests or diversify them is a mathematical optimization over Goal 1 and Goal 2, not a binary rule.

**Recommended solution and why.** After generating candidate portfolios, score each selected lineup `L` on every contest `c`'s specific top-1% prize equity `V_{L,c}` and fee-weighted cash probability `C_{L,c}`. Solve a small bipartite matching / assignment problem (under 50 milliseconds in `scipy.optimize.linear_sum_assignment` or GLPK) that maps lineups to `Entry ID` slots across contests to maximize total fee-weighted Goal 1 plus Goal 2 coverage, subject to zero duplicate lineups within the same contest and a soft penalty on reusing identical lineups across different single-entry contests.

### D-05: Implied-minutes back-solver from free bookmaker player props
**Affects:** Both goals · **Effort:** Low · **Confidence:** High

**What it is.** Instead of treating player props as an optional stat-mean nudge available on only 7 nights a month (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §4 line 349, §10 line 866), pull ESPN's free DraftKings milestone prop ladders nightly (`site.api.espn.com` `propBets`, verified working with 123 items per game in `R4-data-sources.md` §4.4) and **back-solve each player's market-implied minutes** by dividing his prop-implied fantasy points by his shrunk per-minute rate (`FPPM`), then reconciling across the team to 240 minutes.

**Why it's worth solving.** `R1-platforms.md` (Finding 1) verified DARKO's key finding: statistical models beat DFS sites on every per-minute box-score rate, and **lose only on minutes projections**. Sportsbook player-prop traders price shootaround whispers, coaching hints, and injury-replacement minutes into points/rebounds/assists lines hours before casual DFS projection sites update. Back-solving implied minutes `Min_mkt = Prop_Implied_DKFP / Prior_FPPM` turns free sportsbook prop ladders into a live, 30-team minutes prior every night at zero dollar cost.

**Recommended solution and why.** In `Component 8 (`Market anchor`)`, convert ESPN's DraftKings milestone ladders (`15+, 18+, 20+` points, rebounds, assists, threes) into expected stat means via survival-function integration (`E[X] = sum P(X >= k)` after removing bookmaker vig), compute `Prop_Implied_DKFP`, divide by the player's shrunk per-minute rate from `Component 6`, and blend `Min_mkt` (weight `0.50` when props exist) with the historical rotation model before the LLM researcher runs. Automatically flag any player where `|Min_mkt - Min_model| >= 4.0` minutes in the `news_digest.json` so the LLM researcher investigates why the betting market disagrees with our depth chart.

### D-06: Two-speed "Fast News" threshold updater via sparse score deltas
**Affects:** Feasibility, Both goals, Workload · **Effort:** Low · **Confidence:** High

**What it is.** In `R9-night-benchmark.md` (lines 134-148), a narrow late-news re-run took `103 seconds` at `S = 30,000` (`44s` at `S = 10,000`) because **73.1 seconds (`71%` of the time)** went to recomputing the 5-contest Classic and 6-contest Showdown field thresholds from scratch. Replace full field re-scoring during `/news` with **incremental sparse score-delta updates**.

**Why it's worth solving.** When a player in Game `g` is ruled out 20 minutes before tip, the simulated scores of the `~180` players in the *other* 7 games on the slate **do not change at all** (`night_spike.py` keys random draws by game, `line 299`). Re-multiplying all 22,000 opponent Classic lineups across all 208 players and 30,000 scenarios wastes 90% of the matrix work.

**Recommended solution and why.** Cache the `22,000 x S` opponent field scores (or chunked partial sums of the unaffected games `X_other`) during the main afternoon build (`~250 MB` quantized `uint16` in RAM or memory-mapped on disk). When `/news` re-simulates Game `g` (`1.0 second`), add only the `15-player` score delta for Game `g` (`L_field[:, cols_g] @ (X_new[:, cols_g] - X_old[:, cols_g])`) and apply importance weights for the new chalk ownership shift. That cuts threshold recomputation from `73.1 seconds` to **under 4 seconds**, bringing a complete, exact `/news` re-run at `S = 30,000` under **10 seconds total** without needing precomputed replacement sets (**I-04**).

## What holds up
- **The strict LLM-to-solver boundary (`Brief` Problem 9, `ADR-003`):** Having the LLM output schema-validated belief records (availability, minutes, usage) with verified source quotes while deterministic code builds every lineup is the single best architectural decision in the package.
- **One shared stat-level night simulation (`Brief` Problem 3 and 8, `ADR-002`):** Scoring every Classic and Showdown contest on the same simulated game draws is the only mathematically coherent way to capture cross-contest correlation, blowout/overtime tails, and double-double bonuses.
- **Full enumeration for Showdown (`ARCHITECTURE.md` §6, `ADR-006`, `R2` §8.1):** Listing all ~440,000 legal 6-player Showdown lineups in 0.015 seconds and screening them directly eliminates MILP solver overhead and makes Captain tradeoffs exact.
- **Hall's marriage formulation with GLPK for Classic candidates (`ADR-006`, `R2` §7.3):** Replacing player-by-slot binary variables with one binary variable per player plus 31 position-feasibility rows gives a verified 5x to 25x speedup (`2.2 ms` per solve) with identical optimal lineups.
- **Delivery-first validator wall (`Brief` Constraints, `ADR-010`, `ADR-019`):** Generating a legal, injury-scrubbed fallback file before any research step and enforcing that only DraftKings rule validity can block an export directly prevents the blank-entry losses that plagued your prior engines.
